The goal

Proposal, drafted with the owner on 2026-11-14. The sections below are in the owner's words, tidied. The points under Open are not settled. Nothing here is a decision until the owner puts it in the decision log, and then this banner is removed.

Who it is for

Atlas is for one person in a field, testing drones and sensors. Today that person is the owner. Usually one operator controls several Assets. Sometimes there is more than one operator.

Atlas is a platform for experiments. It is not yet a product that has to work every time. The owner will say when that changes.

Atlas is designed around one gateway. Later the owner wants several gateways in play at once, far enough apart that their radio links never overlap, such as two gateways 20 miles apart. Nothing built now should rule that out (Open, point 3).

The problem

No open-source software does this. The software that does is built for militaries.

A ground-control station such as QGroundControl is made for one drone. A radio plugs into a laptop, the laptop shows the drone, and the operator tells the drone to fly here and then there. The station does not give the drone richer work, and it does not bring back the data the owner wants: Observations, Tracks, places, and a picture fused from several Assets.

What Atlas does

  1. It receives data from each Asset about the Asset itself.
  2. It receives the Observations that each Asset makes.
  3. It sends commands to Assets.

Sending data, receiving data, and tasking Assets are the whole point. Every other part of Atlas exists to serve those three.

What Atlas is not

Not settled (Open, point 1). Four limits are already decided elsewhere:

  • Not a flight controller. The Asset app decides motion and the autopilot flies (Charter).
  • Not a radio. Atlas rides on existing radios through Fieldlinks.
  • Not a mission planner. Work is single Tasks, with no multi-step Plans (Design decisions).
  • Not a long-term archive. A run's data leaves Core at Reset.

How we know it works

In-depth simulations of whole operations show that Atlas works, together with tools that make a new simulation quick to write. Simulations owns how they run.

A simulation runs the real code of every part: the real Asset app, the real Core, the real gateway app, and the real Fieldlinks. No real hardware takes part. Only the equipment and the links between the parts are simulated, such as a simulated radio link.

A simulation covers a full operation and everything that can go wrong in one:

  • Assets take off and make Observations.
  • Other Assets are tasked onto those Observations.
  • Fusion makes Tracks, and other Assets are tasked onto those Tracks.
  • A radio link fails, and an Asset flies out of range.
  • Hardware faults.

The simulations run in CI, so every change is checked against whole operations.

That full simulation cannot run yet, because the Asset app, the gateway app, and the Fieldlinks are not built. The proposed build order says what to build next.

The test question

Hold every new idea against one question: is this overcomplicated?

Atlas aims for systems that are simple and elegant. Complexity for its own sake is not worth having. When a hard problem needs complexity, look for the simpler solution first (Proven and lean).

Open

  1. What Atlas is not. The owner is reviewing a list of candidates.
  2. Tasking an Asset onto a Track. Today a command names a position. An operator can send an Asset to where a Track is now. No command makes an Asset keep following a Track as it moves. Which one the goal needs is the owner's call.
  3. Several gateways. The current rule is one site per Core, with one gateway. Core already lets an admin add more than one gateway and records which gateway carries each Asset, so the limit is a rule, not a built-in barrier. Whether to change the rule now or later is the owner's call.
  4. The field. A simulated pass proves nothing about real flight or radios (Charter). Whether the goal also names a field run, such as first flight, is the owner's call.
